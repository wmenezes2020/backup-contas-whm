# Domain adapter: legal and compliance

Applies when the deliverable touches contracts, terms of service, privacy policies, licenses, regulatory requirements, or compliance checks. The loop is unchanged; these definitions replace the coding defaults.

## Minimum evidence set (binding, before any conclusion)

1. **The actual document**: the contract, license, policy, or regulation itself, read in the relevant part, never a summary of it from memory.
2. **The jurisdiction**: which country's or state's rules apply. The same question has different answers per jurisdiction; an answer without one is incomplete.
3. **Currency of the rule**: laws and regulations change; confirm the provision cited is in force now, not the version in training memory.

## Evidence and primary sources

The document's own text > official sources (legislation, regulator guidance, court records) > reputable legal commentary > memory. Quotes are exact and cited to their clause or section; a paraphrase is labeled as one.

## Authority order

Mandatory law of the stated jurisdiction > the document's actual text > the law that
applies only by default > the user's interpretation > general practice.

The law of the place comes first, and this order used to be written the other way
round, with the document on top. That was wrong in a way that matters: a clause
that contradicts a mandatory rule is not binding because someone wrote it down.
It is void or unenforceable, and reporting it as binding tells a person they owe
something they do not owe. Where a rule is default rather than mandatory (it
applies unless the parties agreed otherwise), the document governs, which is why
both sit above anyone's interpretation.

So there are two disagreements to surface, not one:

- **the text and the user's belief disagree** → that disagreement is the finding;
- **the text and a mandatory rule disagree** → name the rule and mark the clause
  as potentially void or unenforceable. Never report it as simply binding, and
  never let it pass in silence because the document says so.

## Verification by observation

- Every cited clause, section, or provision is quoted from the actual text with its location; the reader can find it.
- Every "the law requires" claim names the instrument and jurisdiction, verified current.
- Any clause that appears to contradict a mandatory rule is flagged as potentially
  void or unenforceable, with the rule named. A clause is never reported as
  binding just because it is in the document.
- Obligations, deadlines, and thresholds are exact: numbers, dates, and defined terms as written, not approximated.
- The report states plainly that it is document analysis, not legal advice, and names the point at which a qualified lawyer is needed. This boundary is a feature of the deliverable, not a disclaimer to bury.

## Fraud table (for fable-judge)

| Fraud | Symptom |
|---|---|
| Fabricated citations | clauses, sections, cases, or statutes that do not exist or do not say it |
| Contract over law | an illegal or unenforceable clause reported as binding because the document says so |
| Jurisdiction blur | rules from one jurisdiction applied to another without flagging |
| Paraphrase as quote | reworded text presented inside quotation marks or as "the contract says" |
| Stale law | superseded or repealed provisions cited as current |
| "Standard practice" assertions | obligations invented from convention rather than the text |
| Confidence past the boundary | definitive legal conclusions where only a licensed professional can give one |

## Done, by example

"The contract review is done" means: each risky clause quoted with its location, the jurisdiction stated, current-law checks noted, the open questions listed, and the lawyer-needed line drawn explicitly. Not: "the contract looks standard."
